# Speed-Dating the Evil Brotherhood — Pandemic: Reign of Cthulhu
Martin Gonzalvez


Press enter or click to view image in full size

A Cthulhu game where Things. Escalate. Quickly.
If you read H. P. Lovecraft or play Call of Cthulhu RPGs, you’ll notice that a big part of why this stuff scares you, is that the horrors start out small, then slowly, gradually build to an overwhelming crescendo of terror. A dream here. A ritual there. Strange goings-on reported in the newspapers. A trail of clues that needs to be investigated, tracked down, which eventually, inevitably, leads to horrific discoveries and encounters with unspeakable…things.

When my college gaming group and I played Masks of Nyarlathotep, our campaign was spread out over a series of several weekends. We battled cultists in New York, a djinn in Cairo, various lesser creatures in other places around the world. An encounter with even just one Old One was enough to drive two player characters instantly insane. To my mind, a proper Mythos story or game takes time to develop. Fear grows.

Not so with Pandemic: Reign of Cthulhu. In this game, things are very bad right from the start, and just keep getting worse from there. There are at least a dozen cultists doing bad cultist things all over the five fictional New England towns in this game. Over a typical 30–45 minute game, players will encounter not just one, but up to six Old Ones (encountering the seventh one, big ol’ Cthulhu himself, results in an instant loss). The unrelenting stress and tension of the original Pandemic game is ratcheted up to eleven in RoC.

Far from being Cthulhu Lite, this game is like a Cthulhu speed date. It’s all the worst parts of the Mythos crammed into a sub-one hour play time. It’s a Lovecraft story if it were directed by Michael Bay. BOOM, three more cultists! KA-BLAM, a Shoggoth appears! KA-POW, an Evil Stirs card! KA-BOOM, an Awakening Ritual! FRA-TWASK, does any of this make any sense at all? I don’t have any idea, all I know is that WE HAVE TO DESTROY THAT SHOGGOTH NOW NOW NOW! It’s a sensory overload, wall of sound, over the top, unrelentingly pressure-filled confrontation with the Brotherhood.

At times the experience is a little too much to take. The last time we played, my head felt like a coke can being crushed in the hands of Nyarlathotep himself. As soon as the game ended, it was a huge relief to play a nice, relaxing game of Clank!
